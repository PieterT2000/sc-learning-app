# Design: Catechism Voice — Voice-First Catechism Memorization App

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-04-13

Mode: Builder

## Problem Statement

Existing catechism memorization apps are digital flashcards. You read a question, tap to reveal the answer, maybe swipe to the next card. They digitized the printed page, but catechism was never about reading. "Catechesis" literally means "to teach by sounding down" — it was an oral tradition for 1,500+ years before anyone put it on a card.

The builder wants an app where you pull it up on your phone, the app asks you a question aloud, you answer from memory, and you see exactly which words you got right and which you missed. Not pass/fail. A diff. Like a mirror that shows you precisely where your knowledge is and where the gaps are.

The primary audience is a church group / Bible study. But the retention mechanism is NOT social competition — it's personal spiritual growth. The app should remind users WHY they're learning each catechism answer, connecting the memorization to the meaning. Leaderboards and badges exist but are secondary to the formation experience.

## What Makes This Cool

Three things no existing app combines:

1. **Voice-first interaction.** The app speaks the question, you speak the answer. True hands-free — usable while walking, doing dishes, commuting. This is historically authentic to how catechism was actually taught for centuries.

2. **Visual diff feedback.** Not just "correct/incorrect" but a word-by-word color-coded diff showing exactly what you nailed and what you missed. Green = correct, red = wrong/missing. The diff is a mirror, not a scorecard.

3. **Formation over gamification.** After each answer, a brief "Why This Matters" reflection connects the memorized words to their spiritual meaning. The badges and streaks are present but the real retention comes from feeling yourself grow deeper into your faith.

## Constraints

- **Phone-first PWA.** Must work great on mobile browsers, installable to home screen. No app store gatekeeping.
- **Free/cheap infrastructure.** Supabase free tier for backend. Web Speech API (free, built-in) for STT and TTS.
- **Single catechism first.** Westminster Shorter Catechism (107 Q&As, public domain). No multi-catechism support in v1.
- **Voice accuracy.** Speech-to-text must be accurate enough for catechism vocabulary (theological terms, archaic phrasing).

## Premises

1. **Voice-first is the primary mode.** The app is built around speaking answers out loud, not typing. The AI reads the question aloud via TTS for true hands-free use.
2. **Phone speech-to-text is good enough.** Modern mobile speech recognition can transcribe catechism answers accurately enough to diff against the source text. Validation step: test Web Speech API accuracy against 20 representative catechism answers on iOS Safari and Android Chrome. If word error rate > 15%, evaluate paid alternatives (Deepgram, AssemblyAI).
3. **The diff is the core feedback loop.** Seeing exactly which words you got right vs. wrong (not just pass/fail) is what makes this better than flashcards.
4. **Personal spiritual growth drives retention, not social competition.** The app reminds users WHY each catechism answer matters. Leaderboards and badges are present but secondary. The core emotional loop is: "I'm getting deeper into my faith."
5. **A web app (PWA) is the right form factor.** Shareable via URL, installable to home screen, no app store approval needed.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Weekend Voice Loop (Minimal Viable)
Single-page PWA with Web Speech API for STT, diff-match-patch for visual feedback. One catechism, two modes (Easy/Hard), localStorage for streaks and badges. No backend, no auth, no leaderboards. Ships in 2 days, validates the core loop. Completeness: 4/10.

### Approach B: Full Formation Experience (Chosen)
Next.js PWA with Supabase backend. Three modes: Learning (with whispered prompts), Easy (gist match), Hard (exact words). TTS reads questions aloud for true hands-free. Post-answer "Why This Matters" reflections. Supabase for auth, scores, cross-device sync, and leaderboards. Badge system with streak tiers. 1-2 weeks with CC. Completeness: 8/10.

### Approach C: AI-Powered Catechist
Everything in B plus LLM (Claude API) for semantic grading in Easy mode and personalized spiritual reflections. 2-3 weeks, ongoing API costs, risk of theological inaccuracy. Completeness: 9/10.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B: Full Formation Experience.** It delivers the complete hands-free formation experience without the complexity and cost of per-answer LLM calls. The three-mode progression (Learning -> Easy -> Hard) creates a natural difficulty curve. The "Why This Matters" reflections can be hand-curated per question, which is actually better for theological accuracy than AI-generated content. The AI catechist layer (Approach C) can be added later once the core experience is validated.

### Core Architecture

**Tech Stack:**
- Next.js 14 (App Router) on Vercel (free tier)
- Supabase (auth, Postgres, real-time) on free tier
- Web Speech API for STT (SpeechRecognition) and TTS (SpeechSynthesis)
- diff-match-patch for word-level diff visualization (requires a word-level wrapper — the library operates on characters by default; its wiki documents the word-tokenization technique)
- Tailwind CSS for styling
- next-pwa for PWA manifest + service worker

**Data Model:**
- `catechisms` — id, name, description (e.g., "Westminster Shorter Catechism")
- `questions` — id, catechism_id, number, question_text, answer_text, reflection_text
- `users` — id, email, display_name (Supabase Auth)
- `attempts` — id, user_id, question_id, mode, transcript, score, prompt_count, created_at
- `streaks` — user_id, current_streak, longest_streak, last_attempt_date
- `badges` — id, name, description, icon, threshold_type, threshold_value
- `user_badges` — user_id, badge_id, earned_at
- `groups` — id, name, invite_code, created_by, created_at
- `group_members` — group_id, user_id, joined_at

**Three Modes:**
1. **Learning Mode** — TTS reads the question. User starts answering. If they pause for 5 seconds, recording pauses, TTS whispers the next few words at reduced volume and faster rate, then recording resumes. The whispered words are excluded from the user's transcript. Score reflects how many prompts were needed. Goal: zero prompts.
2. **Easy Mode** — TTS reads the question. User answers. Scoring uses fuzzy matching: lowercase both strings, strip punctuation, apply word stemming (e.g., "glorifying" matches "glorify"), tolerate stopword omissions ("a", "the"). Score = percentage of canonical answer words matched. General gist earns points.
3. **Hard Mode** — TTS reads the question. User answers. Exact word-for-word match via diff-match-patch. Score = (correct_words / total_canonical_words) * 100. Insertions are highlighted but don't reduce score. Deletions and substitutions reduce score. A question is only "passed" at 100%.

**Scoring:**
- Score = (matched_words / total_canonical_words) * 100
- **Easy mode:** Words are matched after lowercasing, stripping punctuation, and stemming. Stopwords ("a", "the", "and") that are missing reduce score by half weight.
- **Hard mode:** Exact word match only. Any deviation (substitution, omission, reordering) is marked wrong.
- **Learning mode:** Base score same as Easy, but a "prompt penalty" is applied: each prompt used reduces the session score by 10 points. Displayed as "85% (2 prompts used)".

**Mastery Definition:**
- A question is **mastered** when the user scores 90%+ on it in 3 separate sessions (non-consecutive is fine).
- Easy mode mastery and Hard mode mastery are tracked independently.

**Streak Rules:**
- A streak day is counted when the user completes at least one full session (5 questions) on a calendar day in their local timezone.
- Missing a full calendar day resets the streak to 0.
- Multiple sessions in the same day count as one streak day (no double-counting).

**Session Flow:**
1. User selects mode -> "Begin Session" (5 questions)
2. TTS reads question aloud, question text displayed on screen
3. Pulsing mic indicator, live transcript appears as user speaks
4. User taps mic to stop recording (Easy/Hard: 5s silence also stops; Learning: 5s silence triggers whisper, 10s silence stops)
5. Diff screen: score circle, color-coded word diff, "Why This Matters" reflection
6. Streak update, badge earned notification if applicable
7. "Next Question" -> repeat until session complete
8. Session summary: overall score, streak status, questions to revisit

**Badge System:**
| Badge | Threshold | Icon |
|-------|-----------|------|
| First Steps | Complete first session | seedling |
| Consistent | 10-day streak | fire |
| Scholar | 25 questions mastered (Easy) | book |
| Devoted | 25-day streak | star |
| Theologian | 50 questions mastered (Hard) | trophy |
| Faithful | 50-day streak | crown |
| Master | All questions in catechism mastered (Hard) | gem |
| Century | 100-day streak | medal |

**Leaderboard:**
- Per-mode leaderboards (Learning / Easy / Hard)
- Scoped to "groups" — a user creates or joins a group via invite code
- Shows: rank, display name, total score, current streak, badges earned
- Lightweight — not the main event, just visible from a menu

**Hands-Free Flow:**
- "Begin Session" starts TTS reading the first question
- Auto-starts recording after TTS finishes
- After user stops speaking (5s silence in Easy/Hard), auto-processes and shows diff
- TTS reads the score aloud ("85 percent") then reads the full "Why This Matters" reflection
- Auto-advances to next question 2 seconds after TTS completes
- Entire session can be completed without touching the screen after initial tap
- Note: hands-free mode is an audio experience. The visual diff is available in the session summary for review afterward. Best suited for walking, dishes, commuting (not driving — don't look at your phone while driving).

**Fallback & Error Handling:**
- If Web Speech API is unavailable (e.g., Firefox, older browsers): show a text input fallback. The diff still works, you just type instead of speak.
- If speech recognition returns empty or garbage (noisy environment): show a "Didn't catch that — try again?" prompt with a retry button and a "Type instead" link.
- If speech recognition fails mid-sentence: preserve what was captured so far, let the user retry or complete via text.
- If TTS is unavailable: display question text only, skip audio playback, proceed with recording.

## Open Questions

1. **"Why This Matters" content source.** Hand-curate all 107 reflections? Use existing commentary? A mix? (Reflections should be 2-3 sentences max, theologically sound, personally resonant.)
2. **Offline support.** How important is offline mode? PWA service workers can cache the app shell and catechism content, but speech recognition typically needs connectivity (Chrome streams audio to Google's servers).
3. **Group management UX.** How do church groups form? Invite codes? Pastor creates group? Link sharing?

## Success Criteria

- **Core loop works on phone.** Open app -> hear question -> speak answer -> see diff -> feel something. Under 3 seconds from "stop speaking" to "see results."
- **Hands-free works end-to-end.** Complete a 5-question session without touching the screen after the initial tap.
- **At least one church group uses it weekly.** Real people, real usage, not just the builder.
- **Streak retention.** Users who start a streak maintain it for 7+ days at least 30% of the time.
- **The "whoa" test.** Someone pulls it up on their phone for the first time and their reaction is surprise, not "oh, another flashcard app."
- **Web Speech API validation.** Word error rate < 15% on 20 representative catechism answers tested on iOS Safari and Android Chrome.

## Reviewer Concerns

Adversarial spec review (score: 6/10 initial, issues fixed below). Remaining items to address during implementation:

1. **Archaic/theological vocabulary in STT.** Words like "effectual calling," "sanctification," "thee/thou/thy" may be misrecognized by Web Speech API. Mitigation: test early (Next Steps #3), consider a post-processing correction map for common theological terms.
2. **diff-match-patch word-level wrapper.** The library is character-level by default. A word-tokenization preprocessing step is required (documented in the library's wiki). Not complex, but not a drop-in.
